# Chess × Jev — P1 fixed-position move quality

**Question.** Once action *legality* is guaranteed (Jev's Choice can only return
a legal move), does Jev choose *better* chess moves than a random or a cheap
heuristic legal-action selector? This holds the board state and the legal-move
menu constant and grades the single move each policy returns, so it isolates
decision quality from the legality guarantee and from full-game dynamics.

**Design.** 200 stratified positions (40 opening / 60 middlegame / 60 tactical /
40 endgame), generated by seeded Stockfish self-play plus constructed endgames
(`positions.py`, seed 0). Every arm sees the identical FEN, side to move and
legal SAN menu (order randomised per position, recorded). Each returned move is
graded by a cached Stockfish 19 oracle at **depth 16** that scores every legal
move (`evaluator.py`); Jev is never shown those scores. One live Jev API call per
position (`jev-latest`), argmax Choice. Arms: `uniform_legal` (random legal),
`heuristic_legal` (cheap fixed rules: mate→capture→check→promote→random), `jev`.

All cited numbers are regenerated and verified from the frozen
`position_scores.jsonl` by `evidence/generate.py --verify`; see
[`evidence/PROVENANCE.md`](evidence/PROVENANCE.md).

> **Follow-up (P2):** [`../p2_20261025/P2_RESULTS.md`](../p2_20261025/P2_RESULTS.md)
> runs two general LLMs over these same positions. Jev turns out to be
> statistically indistinguishable from `gpt-4o` constrained to the legal menu,
> and far below frontier `claude-opus-5-5` — so the move quality below is
> GPT-4-class, not Jev-specific; the legality guarantee is the wrapper's, not
> Jev's.

## Headline

Jev **clearly beats random** on positive move quality but **does not beat ~30
lines of hand-written rules**, and it carries **more downside risk than either**.
It is not uniformly novice — it is **high-variance**: 1-in-5 moves are the
engine's exact best, but over half are blunders.

## Results (200 positions/arm)

cpL = centipawn loss vs the best legal move; means/medians exclude the 22
forced-mate positions (a mate maps to a huge magnitude and would swamp an
average), which are summarised by the missed-mate rate instead.

| arm | illegal | median cpL* | match@1 | match@3 | blunder (≥300cp) | missed mate (n=22) |
|-----|---------|-------------|---------|---------|------------------|--------------------|
| `heuristic_legal` | 0.0% | **105.5** | **26.5%** | 40.0% | **40.5%** | **40.9%** |
| `jev`             | 0.0% | 425.5 | 20.5% | 33.0% | 55.5% | 54.5% |
| `uniform_legal`   | 0.0% | 266.0 | 3.0% | 13.0% | 51.0% | 72.7% |

\*non-mate positions only.

**Paired, same positions, 95% CI (cluster bootstrap by source game):**

| comparison | metric | mean diff | 95% CI | reading |
|------------|--------|-----------|--------|---------|
| jev − uniform | match@1 | **+0.175** | [+0.114, +0.260] | Jev finds the best move far more often — **real knowledge** |
| jev − heuristic | match@1 | −0.060 | [−0.206, **+0.000**] | Jev does **not** exceed the cheap heuristic (CI touches 0) |
| jev − heuristic | blunder | **+0.150** | [+0.097, +0.298] | Jev blunders **significantly more** than the heuristic |

## What this says

1. **Legality is not the discriminator.** All three arms play 0% illegal moves —
   the structural guarantee is a property of the *legal-action wrapper*, which
   any selector can have, not of Jev specifically. (Confirms the pre-registered
   caution: don't claim Jev alone makes illegality impossible.)

2. **Jev encodes genuine chess knowledge.** It matches Stockfish's top move
   20.5% of the time vs 3.0% for random — ~7× chance, CI well clear of zero. It
   is emphatically not playing random legal moves.

3. **…but a cheap heuristic matches or beats it.** The "grab the biggest
   capture / give check / promote" rule, with no model and no search, ties Jev on
   best-move match (26.5% vs 20.5%, CI touches 0) and blunders markedly less
   (40.5% vs 55.5%, CI excludes 0). Jev's learned priors do not clear the bar set
   by a few obvious rules.

4. **High-variance, bimodal profile.** 41/200 moves are the exact best; 111/200
   are blunders (≥300cp). Jev is brilliant-or-blundering rather than consistently
   mediocre — the signature of per-move pattern recognition with **no search to
   catch the downside** (the endgame queen-hang generalises).

5. **Tactics are the sharp failure, and the heuristic exposes it.** On the
   tactical stratum, Jev blunders in **63.3%** of positions and matches best only
   **25.0%**; the heuristic blunders **26.7%** and matches **53.3%**. Where
   calculation should matter most, the searchless selector does worst — and a
   rule that simply takes material does best (tactical positions by construction
   often have a winning capture/check, so this stratum partly rewards the
   heuristic's bias; read it as "Jev fails to see forcing lines", not "the
   heuristic plays strong chess").

6. **Jev's confidence is calibrated — the most useful positive finding.** Split
   Jev's moves by its own Choice confidence:

   | confidence tertile | match@1 | median cpL (non-mate) |
   |--------------------|---------|-----------------------|
   | low (mean ~0.11)   | 12.1%   | 522 |
   | high (mean ~0.28)  | 40.9%   | 22 |

   High-confidence moves are ~3.4× more likely to be the engine's best and lose
   ~24× fewer centipawns. The confidence signal meaningfully predicts move
   quality, so abstaining or escalating on low confidence is a real lever — even
   though absolute confidence is low throughout (range 0.06–0.77).

## Caveats (load-bearing)

- **Single live run, argmax.** One Jev call per position. A prior determinism
  probe found Jev's *argmax* stable but its probabilities noisy in low-margin
  states, so match rates are trustworthy while the exact confidence cut-points
  would wobble on a re-run; the frozen records make the *reported* statistics
  exact.
- **Evaluator = grader, not opponent.** Stockfish 19 depth 16, independent of any
  game opponent; scores cached and never shown to Jev.
- **Tactical stratum is partly capture-solvable** (see point 5); the heuristic's
  edge there is a property of the stimuli, flagged, not a strength claim.
- **Non-mate cpL means are still skewed** by a minority of large but legitimate
  losses (e.g. hanging a queen ≈ 900cp); the **median** is the central-tendency
  metric, means are in `analysis.md` for completeness.

## Reproduce

```bash
RES=llm_rl_scripts/chess/jev/results/primary_20261025
# 1. positions (offline)
python -m llm_rl_scripts.chess.jev.positions --out $RES/positions.jsonl \
  --count 200 --seed 0 --engine-path /opt/homebrew/bin/stockfish
# 2. baselines + eval cache (offline, no API)
python -m llm_rl_scripts.chess.jev.position_eval --positions $RES/positions.jsonl \
  --out $RES/position_scores.jsonl --eval-cache $RES/eval_cache.json \
  --arms uniform_legal,heuristic_legal --eval-depth 16
# 3. live Jev arm (needs TYPESAFE_API_KEY; reuses the warm cache)
python -m llm_rl_scripts.chess.jev.position_eval --positions $RES/positions.jsonl \
  --out $RES/position_scores.jsonl --eval-cache $RES/eval_cache.json --arms jev --eval-depth 16
# 4. analyse + verify cited numbers
python -m llm_rl_scripts.chess.jev.analyze_positions --scores $RES/position_scores.jsonl \
  --report $RES/analysis.md
cd $RES/evidence && python generate.py --verify
```

Run all live steps inside the `LLM_RL` conda env with
`conda run --no-capture-output -n LLM_RL python -u ...`. Numbers from a run on
2026-10-25, `jev-latest`, Stockfish 19, depth-16 grader.
